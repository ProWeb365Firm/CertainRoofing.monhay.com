About Ice Dams, and Why Your Attic Matters More Than Your Roof

Winter isn’t just something we work around, it’s a large part of why we exist. Ice dams, the ridges of ice that form at a roof’s edge and force melting snow back under the shingles, are one of the most common sources of winter roof damage in Minnesota. They’re caused by uneven roof temperatures: heat escaping from the living space warms the upper roof deck while the eaves stay cold, so snow melts, runs down, and refreezes. The fix is rarely just ‘a new roof’, it usually starts with air sealing and attic insulation (Minnesota code calls for R-49 minimum in most attics), which is exactly the kind of interior-meets-exterior problem a contractor who does both sides of the house is built to solve.

Frequently Asked Questions About Roofing Services

1. How long does a typical roof replacement or siding project take to complete?

A straightforward asphalt shingle roof replacement on an average-sized Minnesota home generally takes one to three days, while full siding replacements typically run five to ten days depending on square footage and weather delays. Weather windows matter more in Minnesota than in milder climates, since crews need dry conditions and temperatures above freezing for proper shingle sealing and siding installation.

2. What roofing materials hold up best against Minnesota's freeze-thaw cycles and hail?

Architectural (dimensional) asphalt shingles rated Class 4 for impact resistance perform well against hail and ice-dam stress, and many carry insurance premium discounts specifically because of that rating. Metal roofing and engineered composite shingles are also gaining popularity in the Twin Cities for their durability against repeated freeze-thaw cycling, though they carry a higher upfront cost than standard architectural shingles.

3. How can I tell if my roof has storm or hail damage that isn't obviously visible from the ground?

Hail bruising often shows up as soft spots or granule loss on shingles that only a trained eye can spot during a close physical inspection, since damage frequently doesn’t produce visible leaks for months or years. This is why a professional inspection after any significant hailstorm is worth scheduling even if there’s no obvious leak, particularly since insurance claim windows for storm damage are typically time-limited.

4. What's the difference between a roof repair and knowing when full replacement is the smarter investment?

Repairs make sense for isolated issues like a few damaged shingles or a single flashing leak, but once a roof is past 15-20 years old or shows widespread granule loss and multiple problem areas, repeated repairs often cost more over time than a full replacement. A contractor who inspects the whole roof system, not just the reported leak, should be able to tell you honestly which side of that line your roof falls on.

5. Does my new roof or siding come with a workmanship warranty separate from the manufacturer's material warranty?

Reputable Minnesota roofing contractors typically offer a workmanship warranty on the installation itself, covering issues like improper flashing or ventilation, in addition to whatever product warranty the shingle or siding manufacturer provides. It’s worth asking for both warranty terms in writing before work begins, since manufacturer warranties don’t cover installation errors.

6. How does proper roof ventilation affect the lifespan of my shingles and attic?

Poor attic ventilation traps heat and moisture, which can prematurely age shingles from underneath and contribute to the same ice-dam conditions caused by inadequate insulation. A contractor who evaluates ridge vents, soffit vents, and airflow balance during a roof project, not just the shingles themselves, is addressing the whole system rather than a cosmetic fix.

7. Will a new roof or siding installation increase my home's resale value in the Twin Cities market?

Roof and siding replacements consistently rank among the home improvement projects with the strongest returns on resale value nationally, since buyers and inspectors flag aging roofs and siding as immediate red flags during a sale. In the Twin Cities specifically, a documented, permitted roof replacement can also simplify the home inspection process and reduce buyer negotiating leverage during a sale.
