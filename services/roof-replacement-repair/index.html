Roof repair

Roof Repair in St. Paul

Not every roof problem means you need a new roof. Many problems can be fixed with a smart, well-done repair. Our roof repair service in St. Paul covers:

  • Missing, cracked, or curled shingles
  • Damaged flashing around chimneys, vents, and skylights
  • Worn rubber boots around plumbing pipes
  • Small areas of wind or storm damage
  • Sagging or clogged gutters that push water under shingles
  • Damage left behind by ice dams

We look for the cause of the problem, not just the symptom. Then we show you photos so you can see exactly what we see.

Roof Leak Repair

A small leak can turn into a big problem fast. Water can soak insulation, stain ceilings, rot wood, and lead to mold. If you see a water stain, drips in the attic, or peeling paint near the ceiling, call us right away.

Leaks often start far from where the water shows up inside. Water can run along the wood in your attic before it drips. We trace the leak back to its source, fix it, and check the area around it for other weak spots. If your leak started after a storm, visit our storm and hail damage repair page.

Repair or replace?

Should You Repair or Replace Your Roof?

This is the question we hear most. The answer depends on your roof’s age, how much damage there is, and how long you plan to stay in your home.

Repair

A repair usually makes sense when:

  • Your roof is less than 15 years old
  • The damage is in one small area
  • The rest of the shingles still look good

Replace

A replacement usually makes sense when:

  • Your roof is 20 years old or more
  • You have leaks in more than one spot
  • Shingles are curling, cracking, or losing their granules all over
  • You see sagging or soft spots on the roof
  • You keep paying for repairs year after year

We will give you our honest opinion. If a repair buys you several good years, we will say so.

Replacement

Roof Replacement in St. Paul

When it is time for a new roof, we do it right from the wood deck up. Our roof replacement service in St. Paul includes:

  1. Full tear-off. We remove the old shingles down to the wood. This lets us find rot that a layover would hide.
  2. Deck repair. We replace any soft or damaged boards.
  3. Ice and water shield. We add a waterproof layer along the edges and valleys, where ice dams and leaks start.
  4. Underlayment. A tough layer goes over the rest of the roof to keep water out.
  5. New shingles. We install architectural asphalt shingles, with Class 4 impact-resistant options for extra hail protection.
  6. New flashing and vents. We seal around chimneys, walls, and pipes, and set up proper roof ventilation.
  7. Cleanup and final check. We sweep your yard for nails with a magnet and walk the job with you.

Most roof replacements on a typical home take one to three days, depending on size and weather.

Class 4 Impact-Resistant Shingles

Class 4 rating

Hail is a fact of life in the Twin Cities. Class 4 is the highest impact rating a shingle can get. To earn it, a shingle must survive being hit by a 2-inch steel ball without cracking. Some insurance companies give a discount on your premium when you install them.

Ice dams

Built for Minnesota Winters: Stopping Ice Dams

Ice dams are one of the top causes of roof leaks in Minnesota. Here is how they form. Heat from your home leaks into the attic. That warm air melts snow on the roof. The water runs down to the cold edge of the roof and freezes again. Over time, a ridge of ice builds up. Water gets trapped behind it and seeps under your shingles.

Better shingles alone will not fix this. It takes a whole system:

Attic insulation

Keeps heat in your living space. For Minnesota attics, a good target is about R-49.

Roof ventilation

Pulls cold air in at the soffits and lets warm air out near the peak. This keeps the roof cold.

Ice and water shield

Along the roof edge, it acts as a backup.

Clear gutters

Let melting snow drain away.

When we replace a roof, we check your attic too. Fixing the cause can stop leaks and lower your heating bills.

Roofing FAQ

Still wondering about something? Call (651) 925-9293 and ask.

How much does roof repair cost in St. Paul?

It depends on what is wrong. Fixing a few shingles or one pipe boot costs much less than repairing a large damaged area. We give you a free written estimate after we look at your roof. You will know the price before any work starts.

How long does a new roof last in Minnesota?

Architectural asphalt shingles usually last about 20 to 30 years here. Good installation and good attic ventilation help a roof reach the high end of that range. Hail, ice dams, and poor airflow can shorten it.

Can you put new shingles over my old roof?

Sometimes building code allows it, but we almost always recommend a full tear-off. A layover hides rot and soft spots. It also adds weight and can shorten the life of your new shingles. Tearing off lets us fix problems before they grow.

Do I need a permit for a new roof in St. Paul or Woodbury?

In most cases, yes. Both cities require a permit for a full roof replacement. We take care of the permit for you, so the job meets code and passes inspection.

Can you work on my roof in winter?

Roofing is weather-dependent in Minnesota. Call to discuss your roof and current scheduling; do not assume winter or emergency availability.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for a new roof?

Insurance usually pays for sudden damage, like hail or wind. It does not pay for normal wear and old age. If you think a storm damaged your roof, we will inspect it for free and give you photos to share with your insurance company.

What are the signs I need a new roof?

Look for shingles that curl, crack, or go missing. Check your gutters for piles of sand-like granules. Inside, look for water stains on ceilings or daylight coming through the attic boards. If your roof is over 20 years old and shows any of these signs, it is time for an inspection.

How should I get ready for roof replacement day?

Move cars out of the driveway and take patio furniture, grills, and planters away from the house. Take pictures and mirrors off walls on the top floor, since hammering can shake them. Let your neighbors know we are coming. Keep kids and pets inside while we work, and we will handle the rest.

How long does a roof replacement take?

Most homes take one to three days. Bigger or steeper roofs can take longer. Rain or high winds may push a day back. We keep you updated the whole time.

Are you licensed and insured?

Yes. Certain Roofing is a licensed Minnesota contractor (License #BC80552) and carries full insurance. We are happy to show you proof before any work begins.
