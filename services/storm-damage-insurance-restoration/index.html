After a storm

What to Do Right After a Storm

The first few hours after a storm matter. Here is what we tell every homeowner:

  1. Stay safe. Do not climb on your roof. Watch for downed power lines and broken glass.
  2. Take photos. From the ground, take pictures of your roof, siding, gutters, windows, and yard. Your phone will save the date.
  3. Check inside. Look for water stains on ceilings, wet spots in the attic, or drips around windows.
  4. Protect your things. Put out buckets, move furniture, and cover items that could get wet.
  5. Call for a free inspection. We will check your home and tell you if the damage is worth an insurance claim.
  6. Keep your receipts. Your insurance policy may pay you back for emergency repairs you need right away.

Hail damage

Signs of Hail Damage on Your Roof

Hail damage is hard to see from your yard. A roof can look fine and still be badly hurt. Here are the signs we look for:

Dark spots or "bruises."

Hail knocks off the gritty granules that protect your shingles. The bare spots break down faster in the sun.

Granules in your gutters

If your gutters or downspouts are full of sand-like grit after a storm, your shingles took a hit.

Cracked or split shingles

Large hail can crack shingles all the way through.

Dents in metal

Check your gutters, downspouts, roof vents, and the fins on your air conditioner. Dents there often mean the roof was hit too.

Lifted or missing shingles

Wind can break the seal that holds shingles down, even if they do not blow away.

Even if your roof is not leaking today, hail damage can cut years off its life. Most insurance policies also have a deadline for filing a claim. That is why it pays to have your roof checked soon after a storm.

Wind damage

Signs of Wind Damage

Wind damage can be just as serious as hail, and it is often easier to spot. After a windstorm, walk around your home and look for:

  • Shingles lying in your yard or driveway
  • Bare patches or exposed black paper on the roof
  • Shingles that are creased, bent, or flipped up at the edges
  • Loose or missing ridge caps along the top of the roof
  • Gutters pulled away from the house
  • Siding pieces that are loose, cracked, or missing

Shingles that lift in the wind can lose their seal. Even if they lay back down, they may blow off in the next storm. That is why we check every slope by hand, not just from the ladder.

If you need a new roof after a hailstorm, it is a great time to think about Class 4 impact-resistant shingles. They carry the highest impact rating a shingle can earn. Storm chasers

How to Spot a Storm Chaser

After a big storm, out-of-town crews often go door to door. Some do fine work. Others do poor work, then leave the state. Protect yourself with these tips:

  • Ask for a local address and a Minnesota contractor license. You can look up any license on the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry website.
  • Never sign anything on your doorstep the same day.
  • Be careful of anyone asking for a large cash payment up front.
  • Walk away from anyone who offers to pay your deductible.
  • Ask a lawyer or your insurance contact about cancellation rights, deadlines, and notice requirements if your claim is denied.

Storm Damage FAQ

Still wondering about something? Call (651) 925-9293 and ask.

Will my homeowners insurance cover hail damage?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden damage from hail and wind. They do not cover normal wear and tear. Every policy is different, so check your coverage or ask your agent. We will inspect your roof for free and tell you what we find.

How can I tell if I have hail damage if my roof is not leaking?

Many hail-damaged roofs do not leak right away. Look for dents in gutters, vents, and your air conditioner, and for grit piling up in your downspouts. The best way to know for sure is a free roof inspection. We will show you photos of anything we find.

Should I call my insurance company or a roofer first?

We suggest a free inspection first. A trained roofer can tell you if there is real damage and how much. That way you do not file a claim for damage that is too small, and you know what to report when you do call your insurance company.

Can hail damage my siding and windows too?

Yes. Hail can crack vinyl siding, dent metal trim, chip paint on fiber cement, and break window screens. After a storm, we check your whole home, not just the roof. Everything we find goes into one report for your insurance company.

How long do I have to file a claim?

It depends on your policy. Many policies give you a set amount of time after the storm date. Waiting can also let small damage grow into bigger problems that insurance may not cover. It is best to get an inspection within a few weeks of a storm.

Do you offer emergency roof repair at night or on weekends?

Night and weekend emergency availability has not been confirmed. Posted office hours are Monday through Friday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. Call (651) 925-9293 to ask about current availability; do not assume 24/7 service or a guaranteed response time.

Will filing a claim raise my insurance rates?

It depends on your insurance company and your claims history. Ask your agent before you file. If your damage is small, we will tell you honestly whether a claim makes sense or whether a simple repair is the better choice.

What if my insurance company denies my claim?

First, ask for the denial in writing so you know the reason. Sometimes damage was missed or not clearly shown. We can give you more photos and details to send back. If the claim is still denied, we will give you a fair price for the repair if you choose to pay for it on your own.
